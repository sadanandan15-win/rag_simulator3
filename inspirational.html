Explore His Journey

Early Life & Education

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. Coming from a humble family, young Kalam sold newspapers to support his family while pursuing his education.


Despite financial struggles, he remained deeply passionate about science and learning. He studied physics at St. Joseph’s College, Tiruchirappalli, and later pursued aerospace engineering at the Madras Institute of Technology.


His childhood discipline, simplicity, and determination shaped him into one of the most respected scientists and leaders in India.

Career in Space & Defence

1960 — Joined DRDO

Dr. Kalam began his career at the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO), where he worked on aeronautical projects.

1969 — ISRO & Satellite Launch Vehicle

He joined the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and became the Project Director for India's first Satellite Launch Vehicle (SLV-III).

1980 — Rohini Satellite Launch

Under his leadership, India successfully deployed the Rohini satellite into orbit, making India a space-capable nation.

Integrated Guided Missile Programme

Dr. Kalam led India's missile development programme and contributed to missiles like Agni and Prithvi, earning the title “Missile Man of India.”

Major Achievements

Pokhran-II Nuclear Tests

Played a critical role in India's successful nuclear tests conducted in 1998, strengthening national security.

Vision 2020

Developed a transformative vision for India to become a developed nation through technology, innovation, and education.

Scientific Leadership

Guided India's aerospace and defence sectors with unmatched leadership and strategic thinking.

Youth Inspiration

Inspired millions of students through speeches, books, and interactions focused on dreams and nation-building.

The People's President

Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007. Unlike traditional politicians, he connected deeply with students and common people.


His Presidency was marked by humility, transparency, and a futuristic vision for India. He was affectionately known as “The People's President” because of his approachable nature and inspiring personality.


Even after completing his term, he continued traveling across the country delivering lectures and motivating youth.

Universities across the world honored him with honorary doctorates and recognitions.

Legacy & Inspiration

Dr. Kalam believed that the youth of India had the power to transform the nation. He continuously emphasized innovation, education, leadership, and integrity.


On 27 July 2015, while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong, Dr. Kalam collapsed and passed away doing what he loved most — teaching students.


His life remains a symbol of hard work, simplicity, patriotism, and relentless pursuit of knowledge.
